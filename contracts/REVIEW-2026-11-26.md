# internal review / Backstop + FeeRouter (Stage C), 2026-11-26

NOT an audit. Written by the same tooling that built the contracts, which is exactly why an independent
audit is still required. Scope: `contracts/src/Backstop.sol` (0xc5b6CA155E17a4Ff45ffC056Ff39468d48940409) and
`contracts/src/FeeRouter.sol` (0xFad19ece5eB90cC4442589541fa3Be9d0853169c), both live on Base since block
62,181,865, neither upgradeable, neither with an owner who can pause.

## B1 / medium / an empty poke resets the drag clock (CONFIRMED, PoC in test/StageC.t.sol)

`unlockCallback` sets `movedUpAt = block.timestamp` whenever the clamped `next` is under `lower`, even when no
band is then placed (balance under `MIN_ETH`). `lower` only moves when a band is placed. So while the Backstop
holds no band, anyone who pokes once a day keeps resetting the clock and the band never follows a rising price:
`test_review_emptyPokeResetsTheDragClock` pumps, pokes empty five days running, funds it, and the first band is
still at the deployment anchor.

- impact: no funds lost. The buyback sits at the anchor (tick 191400) and does nothing until the price falls
  back to it. Griefing costs the attacker only gas.
- when it applies: only while `liquidity == 0` (before the first funded poke, and after a band is bought out
  completely with less than `MIN_ETH` left).
- mitigation now: keep a band placed. Fund the Backstop with at least 0.001 ETH and let the keeper poke; while a
  band is placed the clock only resets when the band really moves.
- fix (needs a new Backstop, it cannot be patched): reset `movedUpAt` only in the branch that places a band, and
  let `lower` follow there.

## B2 / low / a dump-poke-rebuy parks the band low

A large sell pushes the tick up; a poke follows a falling coin price at once; the seller buys back and the band
now sits far under the market, climbing back one spacing (~2%) per day. The attacker pays the pool fee both
ways and the old band buys part of the dump (burnt), so it is griefing at a cost, not a theft. Accepted
trade-off of the anti-pump drag; a future version could follow up faster when the band is far (e.g. N spacings
per day by distance).

## B3 / low / ticks near the edge

If the coin price collapses toward zero, `next` can approach `TickMath.MAX_TICK` and `getSqrtPriceAtTick` would
revert, after which a placed band cannot be taken out (ETH locked). Only reachable with a worthless coin.
A version 2 should clamp `next` to `maxUsableTick`.

## F1 / info / one refusing payee stops the split

`distribute([ETH])` reverts entirely if the treasury or the Backstop refuses ETH. Both accept today (the
treasury is an EIP-7702 wallet with a payable delegate, the Backstop has `receive`). ETH waits, nothing is lost.

## F2 / info / the treasury's powers

The treasury alone can `setReferrer` and, after a public 7-day `announceMove`, propose a new protocol
recipient. That is the escape hatch the owner chose; it means a stolen treasury key can redirect future protocol
fees after 7 visible days. It cannot touch the Backstop's ETH or the creators' 80%.

## checked and fine

- the Backstop never swaps, holds no approvals, has no admin, and only a seller of the coin can move its ETH.
- ETH accounting in `unlockCallback`: band out + held balance, settled or taken as one net delta; coins taken
  and sent to `0x...dEaD` in the same poke.
- reentrancy: both entry points locked; `unlockCallback` refuses any caller but the PoolManager.
- the router sends tokens with a return-value check that accepts no-return tokens and refuses `false`.
- `accept`, `pullOwed`, `distribute` are permissionless and can only pay the fixed destinations.

## what an external audit should look at first

B1 and B3 (both need a new Backstop), the v4 rounding in `getLiquidityForAmount0` against the hook's dynamic fee,
the drag/anchor economics under a real attacker with capital, and the factory interaction (`collect` pushing ETH
to the router with no gas stipend).
